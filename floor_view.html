Occupied rooms: temperature, day versus night

Rooms with an occupancy sensor and trended setpoints (2nd floor); the room temperature dots appear only where a zone temperature is trended. Green bar: share of weekday daytime hours (06:00 to 18:00) with presence detected. Dots: average temperature, day (orange) and night (blue). Amber bar: the daytime setpoint band. Rooms at the top are rarely occupied yet held to the same band.

Daytime setpoint opportunity: 7 of 20 rooms were occupied in under 30% of weekday daytime hours, yet their daytime band averages 69.5 to 74.3 F. Widening their daytime band (or letting the sensor drive an unoccupied setback) is the candidate change. Motion sensors can miss still people, so check a room before changing it.
How to read the opportunity: the gap between the night setpoint band and the unoccupied target is how far the setpoints could be relaxed. A common rule of thumb is about 1% of heating or cooling energy per degree F of setback over 8 hours, so the saving is indicative until it is checked against metered use. Trend data stays on the building's side of the model; no occupancy or schedule data was used, so confirm that nobody works at night before applying it.
2nd Floor: 24 zone units are modeled and their setpoints are trended, but no zone temperature sensor series is linked, so comfort cannot be shown yet. Linking or trending the zone temperature points would add this floor to the view.

Executive summary

Only the observations that matter. Source: the model, the trend database (28 days to 2026-10-07, a shoulder-season window) and the analyses already recorded in the 223P summary report.

Positive findings

  • Zone comfort is good: 99% of occupied hours are within 1 F of setpoint across 20 first-floor zones.
  • No heating while zones were warm: 0.0 of 115.5 heat-on hours had the zone above 74 F (5 first-floor PIUs).
  • No override was left on: 24 override events on 12 irrigation valve timers, 235 minutes in total, the longest 30 minutes, none since June 9.
  • Reheat energy is small: about 78 kWh for 7 second-floor PIUs from April 12 to May 10, and almost none in September to October.
  • The building follows its schedule: occupancy is near zero at night and all weekend, and airflow at night is 37% of daytime.

Anomalies and gaps

  • No night setback: average zone temperature is 72.0 F by day and 72.2 F at night, with a wide night band, so the building floats rather than being set back.
  • 7 of 20 second-floor rooms were occupied in under 30% of weekday daytime hours but are held to a daytime band of about 69.5 to 74.3 F. A wider daytime band is a candidate, but the saving cannot be estimated: no electric or heating-energy meter is trended and none can be associated with these zones.
  • Two PIUs reheat much more than the others: piu_3 (about 42 kWh) and piu_20 (about 26 kWh) in April to May. Worth a check of their setpoints and sensors.
  • 19 of 20 room occupancy sensors reported at least one detection at night (7% of detections). Cause not identified; could be cleaning or sensor noise.
  • The second floor has no zone temperature series, so its comfort cannot be shown.
  • The building occupancy point has no documented unit (whole numbers 0 to 4).

What the model added

  • Meaning: Brick and 223P classes tell a zone temperature from a setpoint or a discharge temperature, so the same views read any floor without name matching.
  • Relationships: equipment is tied to its floor, room and serving air handler, which joined room, sensor, setpoint and temperature in one view.
  • Gaps in one place: missing zone temperature, undocumented units and untrended meters show up as readiness gaps, not as silent empty queries.
Caution: this is one 28-day shoulder-season window and the savings are not quantified. Links between trend series and equipment are partly name-based and unreviewed, and room assignments are mostly medium confidence. The model gives structure and makes these questions quick to ask; the findings still need review by someone who knows the building, and none of the energy figures should be quoted as a saving until checked against metered use.
